# Creative Brief: Stevin Hero Loop — "The Signal in the Noise"

## 1. Project Overview

**Client:** Stevin (stevin.ai)
**Deliverable:** Loopable hero video for a premium AI data platform (SaaS)
**Placement:** Full-width hero section on the homepage. Autoplays muted, no controls visible.

**The Product:** Stevin connects 170+ data sources (ads, social, streaming, e-commerce, CRM) into one AI-driven platform. It serves two worlds: marketing agencies managing multiple clients, and artists/influencers tracking cross-channel momentum. The core promise: we turn noise into signal.

**The Feeling:** Controlled chaos. The world is drowning in fragmented data — notifications, metrics, dashboards, alerts. Stevin is the moment it all snaps into focus. Think: the deep breath after the storm.

**Reference Tone:** Stripe's product videos. Linear's launch trailers. Not corporate. Not cute. Precise, fast, premium.

## 4. The Visual Flow — 4 Acts

The entire video is one continuous motion. There are no hard cuts to black. Instead, **data particles are the connective tissue** — they morph, travel, and reshape between scenes. Think of them as a visual thread being pulled through three different worlds before it's finally woven into order.

### Act 1: The Creator (seconds 0-3)
**Setting:** A DJ behind decks, a festival crowd. Hands in the air. Strobes. Energy at maximum. Use stock footage — dark, high-contrast, shot from behind/above the DJ looking out at the crowd.

**Motion Graphics Layer:** As the crowd surges, abstract UI elements erupt from the mass — stylized like/heart icons, mention counters, waveform visualizations, follower tickers. Not literal Instagram UI, but *abstracted* versions: clean lines, the brand blue and pink, floating upward like embers from a fire. The data is wild, uncontrolled, beautiful chaos.

**Key Visual:** Hundreds of smartphone screens lighting up in the crowd, each one emitting a thin stream of data particles (blue/pink) that rise and scatter.

**Transition out:** The particles don't disappear — they accelerate horizontally off-screen to the right, pulling us into Act 2.

---

### Act 2: The E-Commerce (seconds 3-6)
**Setting:** Tight shot. A finger on a trackpad. An abstract, minimal product page (no real brand). A cursor moves. Use stock footage — laptop on a clean desk, natural light, modern workspace.

**Motion Graphics Layer:** The particle stream from Act 1 rushes in from the left and restructures into e-commerce data: an abstract shopping cart filling, a conversion pixel firing (visualized as a sharp teal flash), order confirmation lines streaming across the screen like a receipt printing at high speed. Revenue counters tick upward in brand teal.

**Key Visual:** The moment of conversion — a single decisive click, and the data responds instantly. Pixel fires. The particles are now organized into clean horizontal lines, like data flowing through a pipeline.

**Transition out:** The organized data lines compress into a tight stream and dive downward into darkness.

---

### Act 3: The Business (seconds 6-9)
**Setting:** A darker, more serious environment. A founder picks up a phone from a conference table. Or: a wide shot of a warehouse/office at night, screens glowing. Stock footage — moody, low-key lighting, no smiling faces.

**Motion Graphics Layer:** The data stream enters from above and splits. Some streams turn red/pink — these represent waste: budget leaking, missed leads, broken tracking. They visually collide with barriers (thin teal lines that snap into place like shields), blocking the waste. Meanwhile, green/teal streams (qualified leads, healthy margins) accelerate through, growing stronger. KPI numbers flash: CPA dropping, ROAS climbing.

**Key Visual:** The contrast between red (waste, blocked) and teal (signal, accelerating). The visual language of *filtering* — not everything gets through, only what matters.

**Transition out:** All remaining particles — red and teal — suddenly freeze mid-air. A half-second of absolute stillness.

---

### Act 4: The Signal (seconds 9-12)
**Setting:** Pure black (#0A1628). No stock footage. Full motion graphics.

**The Snap:** Every frozen particle from Act 3 is violently pulled toward the center of the screen in a single frame — like a black hole collapsing. A brief, bright flash of teal (#00D4A0).

**The Reveal:** From that center point, a clean, minimal dashboard unfolds. Not a screenshot — an *abstracted* version: clean cards, subtle grid lines, a few KPI numbers ticking into place (in brand blue and teal), a small chart drawing itself. It breathes. It's calm. It's the eye of the storm.

No logo. No text. Just the dashboard breathing.

**Hold for 2-3 seconds**, then the dashboard elements gently begin to dissolve back into scattered particles — which accelerates back into Act 1's crowd chaos, completing the loop.

## 5. What We're NOT Looking For

- Corporate stock footage cliches (handshakes, people pointing at whiteboards, diverse team laughing)
- Literal UI screenshots or screen recordings
- Slow, cinematic pacing — this needs to feel fast and rhythmic
- Voice-over or text overlays (the site handles all copy)
- 3D logo animations or spinning globes
- Any audio track embedded in the deliverable
- Any logos, brand names, or text overlays — the video must work as a pure visual piece

1. **Storyboard / Styleframe deck** (4-6 key frames showing the look before animation begins) — for approval before production starts

5. **Poster frame** — A single high-res still (PNG) for the video's poster/thumbnail before it loads
